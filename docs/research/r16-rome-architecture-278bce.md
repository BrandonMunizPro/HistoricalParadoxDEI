# Republican Rome — architectural reference sheet (278 BCE)

**Status: Proposal (2026-10-14), part of R-16 (settlement sub-program).** A
reference sheet for rendering Rome *in the mid-Republican period* — **not**
imperial Rome. Scope: the city of Rome and, by extension, what a central Italian
community may plausibly look like. Confidence: **[D]** directly supported ·
**[R]** reconstructed · **[S]** speculative.

## 1. Context at 278 BCE

- Rome is the **hegemon of peninsular Italy** through the *socii* system, not yet
  a Mediterranean empire; its first overseas provinces (Sicily, 241) lie in the
  future (`r16-rome-278bce.md`; the comparative study's anachronism guards).
- **Consuls:** C. Fabricius Luscinus II and Q. Aemilius Papus II. **The denarius
  does not exist** (from ~211); period coinage is the didrachm/quadriga type.
- The city is already large and walled, but **materially modest**: tuff, timber,
  terracotta and mud brick. **Marble, travertine and concrete are not in use**
  (see register).

## 2. City form and topography (morphology)

| Feature | 278 BCE reality | Class |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Situation | Cluster of hills around the Tiber, with the Forum in the valley between | [D] |
| Street pattern | **Organic/irregular**, not a grid; a few planned alignments | [D] |
| City wall | The tuff **ashlar circuit** (conventionally called "Servian"), built after the Gallic sack (early 4th c. BCE) | [D] |
| Gates | Named gates in the circuit (e.g. Porta Capena, Porta Esquilina); simple, mostly unmonumental | [D/R] |
| River | The Tiber, with the **Pons Sublicius** (wooden, oldest bridge) and the river port; the **Cloaca Maxima** drain | [D/R] |
| Hills | Palatine, Capitoline, Esquiline, Aventine, Caelian, etc. — settlements on and between them | [D] |
| Civic core | The **Forum Romanum** — the political and religious heart | [D] |

## 3. Principal structures plausibly present at 278 BCE

| Structure | Description | Class |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Temple of Jupiter Optimus Maximus** (Capitoline) | The great state temple; Italic/Etruscan form — high podium, deep porch, frontal staircase, Tuscan columns, terracotta decoration | [D] |
| **Comitium / Curia Hostilia** | Open voting/assembly space and the Senate house in the Forum (Senate house rebuilt much later by Sulla — the 278 form is earlier/smaller) | [R] |
| **Rostra** | Speaker's platform in the Forum (monumental form is later; earlier is modest) | [R] |
| **Temple of Saturn** | In the Forum, associated with the state treasury (*aerarium*) | [D/R] |
| **Temple of Castor and Pollux** | Dated to the early Republic (dedicated 484 tradition) | [D/R] |
| **Temple of Concord** | Dated to 367 BCE | [R] |
| **Forum Boarium / river temples** | Port/river sacred area; the surviving round temple and the Temple of Portunus in their **current** form are later reconstructions | [R] |
| **Capitol** | Citadel + great temple; the "geo-political" high point | [D] |
| **Regia / Vestal precinct** | Archaic sacred complex in the Forum (rebuilt over time) | [R] |
| **Houses** | **Atrium domus** on the Palatine/Esquiline; courtyard houses; *tabernae* (booths/workshops) | [R] |
| **Aqueduct** | **Aqua Appia** (312 BCE) — present; **no arcades** (it ran underground) | [D for existence; arcades later] |

## 4. Building forms and materials (Roman/Italic)

- **Walls:** *opus quadratum* — squared **tuff** blocks (the dominant monumental
  material); mud brick and timber framing for ordinary buildings; lime plaster
  and stucco finishes [D/R]. **No** Roman concrete (*opus caementicium*), no
  travertine, no marble monuments [D — negative].
- **Roofs:** pitched, **terracotta tiles**; terracotta architectural decoration
  and revetments on temples; thatch on poorer rural buildings [D/R].
- **Temples:** the **Italic/Tuscan** type — high podium, frontal axis, deep
  porch, wide-spaced Tuscan columns, often with a triple-cella plan [D].
  Increasing Greek formal influence is plausible but the **Tuscan podium temple**
  is the Roman signature [D/R].
- **Public buildings:** the Senate house, open assembly areas, and the Forum as a
  paved/defined civic space. **No basilica** (a late-2nd-c. BCE form) [D —
  negative]. **No permanent stone theatre or amphitheatre** (gladiatorial and
  theatrical displays happen in the Forum/Circus) [D/R].
- **Houses:** **atrium** plan is the Roman signature; single-storey or low;
  grouped around courtyards; **no multistorey apartment blocks (*insulae*)**,
  which are 2nd-c. BCE and later [D/R].

## 5. Development ladders applicable to Rome and central Italy

- **Fortification:** ashlar circuit [D] → tower/reinforcement → later extensions
  (Rome's Republican wall extensions occur in later centuries).
- **Civic:** Forum definition → permanent Senate house → monumental paving and
  porticoes (much of the monumental Forum is later).
- **Religious:** podium temple → temple precinct → terraced sanctuary
  (Capitoline = apex).
- **Residential:** courtyard/atrium houses → larger elite *domus* (the grand
  Republican *domus* flourishes later, 2nd–1st c. BCE).
- **Infrastructure:** bridge, drain, aqueduct, roads (Appian Way 312) — the
  engineering layer visible at 278 is functional, not arcaded/monumental.

## 6. Anachronism guards (do not render at 278)

- **No** Imperial Fora, Colosseum/Flavian amphitheatre, Pantheon, imperial baths,
  Aurelian Walls, imperial palaces, triumphal arches.
- **No** concrete, travertine, or widespread marble.
- **No** multistorey *insulae*, **no basilica**, **no permanent stone theatre or
  amphitheatre**.
- **No** denarius imagery (use period didrachm/quadriga types).
- **No** medieval or Renaissance forms. See
  [`settlement-architecture-evidence-and-anachronism-register.md`](settlement-architecture-evidence-and-anachronism-register.md).

## 7. Procedural component mapping (for Rome and Italic towns)

| Component family | Rome/Italic variant | Reuse |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fortification segment | Tuff ashlar curtain + simple gate | All Latin/Italic towns |
| Temple | Italic podium, Tuscan columns, terracotta revetment | Central Italy, Etruscan |
| Civic shell | Open Forum/assembly, Senate/council hall, speaker platform | Latin colonies, allied towns |
| Dwelling | Atrium domus, courtyard house, *taberna* | Italy broadly |
| Infrastructure | Bridge, drain, paved civic space, non-arcaded aqueduct | Italy broadly |
| Agricultural | Farmstead, field, orchard, threshing floor (no *villa* proper yet) | Italy hinterland |

## 8. Evidence and uncertainty

- **Directly supported:** the tuff circuit's material class and the Tuscan podium
  temple form; the Forum's role; the Appian Way and Aqua Appia; the Tiber bridge
  category. [D]
- **Reconstructed:** the 278 state of individual Forum buildings, the Curia's
  size, the exact house plans. [R]
- **Speculative:** colours, roof hues, street widths, unexcavated sanctuaries,
  and the exact density of housing. [S]
- **Dating caution:** many surviving structures are later rebuilds; a standing
  building is not automatically a 278 building. This mirrors the Norba caution in
  VS-5 (`geographic-fidelity-and-development-visuals.md` §Phase 3).
- **Source basis:** the existing VS-5 Rome sample and its Servian-wall evidence
  link; standard Republican archaeology (tuff ashlar, Tuscan temple, atrium
  house chronology). No new archaeology is performed here.
