# Settlement architecture evidence and anachronism register (278 BCE)

**Status: Proposal (2026-10-14), part of R-16 (settlement sub-program).** The
shared **evidence-class** and **chronology-guard** register for settlement
architecture and visual identity. It exists so that development-driven settlement
art is built from **labelled** evidence and so that later architecture cannot
silently enter 278 BCE. Living register: new findings append rows.

Related: [`r16-settlement-architecture-and-visual-identity.md`](r16-settlement-architecture-and-visual-identity.md),

## 1. Evidence classes

| Class | Meaning | Consequence for art |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[D] Directly supported** | Surviving remains and/or secure dating place the form at or near 278 BCE | Render; keep source metadata |
| **[R] Reconstructed** | The building *type* is known for the culture/period, but this settlement's specific form is inferred from later/analogous examples | Render as plausible; label reconstructed internally |
| **[S] Speculative** | Artistic plausibility, no direct evidence | Allowed only as atmospheric detail; never presented as excavated fact |
| **[X] Anachronistic** | Introduced later than 278 BCE | **Must not appear** (see §3) |

## 3. Anachronism register ([X] — must not appear at 278 BCE)

| Anachronism | Introduced | Correct 278 handling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Roman concrete (*opus caementicium*)** | ~2nd c. BCE (significant) | Use tuff ashlar, mud brick, timber |
| **Travertine** as a standard material | ~2nd c. BCE onward | Use tuff and local stone |
| **Widespread monumental marble** in Roman buildings | ~2nd–1st c. BCE | Marble is Hellenistic, not Republican-Roman norm |
| **Basilica** | late 2nd c. BCE | Use open Forum/portico, no basilica |
| **Multistorey *insulae*** | 2nd c. BCE+ | Single-storey atrium/courtyard *domus* |
| **Imperial fora; Colosseum/Flavian amphitheatre; imperial baths; Aurelian Walls; imperial palaces; triumphal arches; Pantheon** | imperial era | Omit entirely |
| **Permanent stone theatre/amphitheatre in Rome** | late Republican/imperial | Use Forum/Circus for spectacles |
| **Denarius imagery** | ~211 BCE | Use didrachm/quadriga period coinage |
| **Great Altar of Pergamon; high Pergamene gigantomachy monuments** | ~2nd c. BCE | Omit |
| **Fully developed colonnaded streets** as default | later Hellenistic–Roman | Use sparingly, label [R] |
| **Roman Carthage** (post-146 monumental city) | 146 BCE+ | Use Punic Carthage ([R]) |
| **Canonical Carthaginian *cothon* / triple land wall** | later-3rd/2nd c. descriptions | Label [R]; not 278 fact |
| **Ptolemaic giant temples (Edfu, Dendera) in completed form** | largely after 278 (Edfu from 237) | Show ongoing/partial only; label [R] |
| **Ashokan pillars/edicts; monumental Sanchi-type stupas; Northern Black Polished Ware** | from ~268 BCE / later | Omit at 278 |
| **Medieval/Romanesque/Gothic churches; crenellations/machicolations; gunpowder bastions; castles** | medieval/modern | Omit |
| **Modern city plans, grid with modern infrastructure** | modern | Omit |
| **Latrines/sewers/monuments in Roman-imperial form** | imperial | Use only functional, non-monumental forms |

## 4. Positive chronology guardrails

- **Republican Rome is tuff-and-timber**, not marble; monumental engineering
  (Aqua Appia 312, Via Appia 312) is **functional, not arcaded** at 278.
- The **most advanced large-scale planning at 278 is the Hellenistic planned
  city**, not imperial Rome.
- **Punic and Greek worlds carry the monumental stone tradition** at 278.
- **Greek temple orders** (Doric/Ionic; Corinthian increasing) are appropriate;
  the **Tuscan/Italic podium temple** is the Roman signature.
- **Pharos of Alexandria (~280)** is newly/just completed — acceptable but [R].
- **Celtic hilltop *oppida* and *murus gallicus* are later**; at 278 use
  earth-timber ramparts and open/hilltop settlements [R/S].

## 5. Dating cautions (multi-phase sites)

- A surviving wall, gate or temple **does not date every phase to 278**; do not
  flatten multi-phase sites into one date. Precedent: the VS-5 **Norba** note
  against assigning every wall/gate phase to 278
  (`../vs5/geographic-fidelity-and-development-visuals.md` §Phase 3).
- **Pleiades sometimes combines successive cities** in one record
  (Sybaris/Thurii/Copia); reconcile phases separately and never reuse a destroyed
  identity (`../vs5/world-scope-and-settlement-atlas.md`).
- **Harbour/coastline change** (Tiber delta, Ostia accretion, Alexandria's
  ancient shore, Punic harbours) means modern coastlines are not 278 coastlines.

## 6. Open verification gaps

- Per-settlement morphological evidence for the ~700 set (large R-16/R-7
  build-out).
- Ptolemaic temple completion chronology around 278 (Edfu/Dendera).
- Carthaginian fortification/harbour chronology at 278.
- Gallic/Iberian/Mauryan architectural detail (thin record; likely
  representative-pattern treatment).
- Colour/plaster/roofing evidence for most traditions (mostly [S]).

## 7. Provenance and licensing

- No Rome II/DeI/Attila or other proprietary assets, maps or code may be imported;
  those games are leads only (comparative-study policy).
- Gazetteer references (e.g. Pleiades, CC BY 3.0, contributors retained) are the
  accepted place-evidence base; archaeological/primary sources carry their own
  citations.
- Asset-level provenance and licence are tracked per the
  [artwork production roadmap](../presentation/artwork-production-roadmap.md) §9.

> This register is a **living guard**, not a completed survey. It exists so that
