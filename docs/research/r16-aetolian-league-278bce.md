## A. Record header

- **Polity:** the **Aetolian League** (*koinon tōn Aitōlōn*), a **confederation** (federal league) of Aetolian tribes and later of affiliated communities/cities.
- **Date of record:** **278 BCE**. Chronological note: the Gallic invasion culminated at Delphi in late 279 BCE; the Aetolians played a major role in harrying the retreating Gauls (the “Kokkalia” phase) and the league consolidated its position in central Greece by 278. Pyrrhus was in Sicily in 278. No abrupt constitutional reform is attested at 278; institutions are the 4th–early 3rd century federal structure.
- **Government summary (one sentence):** a **federal league** (decision bodies: general assembly and a council) with annually elected federal magistrates headed by a **strategos**, in which member communities retained substantial autonomy; the central authority was corporate/federal (not a monarchy).
- **Territorial/structural scope at the date:** Aetolia proper + federated communities outside Aetolia (a “Greater Aetolia”) with varying degrees of integration; Thermos remained the principal meeting place. The league had **two regular assemblies per year** (Thermica/autumn and Panaetolica/spring) and could convene emergency sessions.
- **Evidence base:** literary (Polybius, Livy, Diodorus, Justin, Pausanias) — much later than 278; inscriptions (federal/decree epigraphy, 3rd–2nd century BC) provide the institutional form but many details postdate 278. **Date-drift warning:** reconstructive in places; many “classical” descriptions reflect the mid-late 3rd century.
- **Sheet author/date:** R-16 research, 2026-10-14.

### C.1 Per-office detail (key)

- **Strategos:** presides, commands, receives embassies. Not an absolute monarch; power checked by council/assembly and annual rotation. Interval rule for re-election attested later (Polybius/Livy) — **not firmly attested at 278**.
- **Hipparchos/others:** the “core three” (strategos, hipparchos, grammateus) appear early; the larger boards appear to grow with league expansion (bureaucratic expansion over time) — at **278 BCE the full later complement is uncertain**.

## D. Selection mechanisms

- **Assembly election (annual):** federal magistrates elected at **Thermica (autumn)**. Method not unambiguously attested at 278 (bloc/division vs. lot/open vote). **Secret ballot anachronism:** none attested at this date. **Confidence: Reconstructed**.
- **Council representation:** member communities send delegates **in proportion to size/population**. **Attested** (institutional principle) — precise proportional formula at 278 **Uncertain**.
- **Apokletoi:** selected from council; method unclear at 278 (**Uncertain / gap**).
- **Member communities:** choose their own local officers/magistrates; selection varies by community (**Attested**, local).

## E. Tenure, terms and re-entry

- **Annual tenure** for all federal magistrates. **Attested**.
- **Re-election possible** in principle, but some sources suggest an **interval** before repeating the strategos office (probably 4 years later) — this is attested in later practice; **explicit rule at 278 BCE is not attested**. Marked **Uncertain / gap**.
- **Rotation/accountability:** annual terms + assembly oversight. **Reconstructed**.
- **Term limits (hard):** none attested at 278. **Uncertain / gap**.

## G. Institutional checks

- **Corporate checks:** assembly is sovereign; council prepares and constrains agenda; **apokletoi** limited to day-to-day. Power flows through **bodies**, not concentrated in an uncheckable individual (contrast pure monarchy). **Attested** (principle).
- **Annual accountability:** all magistrates face re-election/oversight annually. **Attested**.
- **Pro-bouleutic council:** assembly business typically filtered through council — a practical check on hasty action. **Reconstructed**.
- **Delegation to member communities:** substantial internal autonomy limits federal reach; league could be **unable/unwilling** to prevent member actions against treaty partners (documented later) — **operational constraint**, not a formal veto. **Reconstructed**.

## H. Military command authority

- **Unified command in war (strategos):** strategos is chief federal commander. **Attested**.
- **Federal levy:** member communities contribute contingents (troops/cavalry) under their own officers but under federal strategos’ command when operating as the league army. **Attested**.
- **Civil/military separation at federal level:** strategos is military + executive; distinct from purely civil offices but fused in the chief magistrate. **Reconstructed**.
- **Campaign flexibility:** league armies composite (Aetolians + federated allies). **Attested**.
- **Note 279–278 context:** Aetolians fielded large contingents against the Gauls (Thermopylae aftermath, pursuit of Acichorius, operations around Delphi/Kokkalia). Command structure at that moment fits **strategos-led federal levy**. **Confidence: Reconstructed**.

## I. Financial authority

- **Federal treasury distinct from member treasuries:** tamiai (later attested) administer league funds. **Reconstructed**.
- **Revenue sources:** contributions from member communities (proportionate), booty, fines, possibly harbor/trade levies? Evidence at 278 **Uncertain / gap**.
- **Spending authority:** assembly/council authorise; tamiai disburse. **Reconstructed**.
- **No single “royal treasury”:** corporate (league) funds, not personal. **Reconstructed**.

## J. Informal political influence

- **Elite families dominate federal offices** (the strategoi typically come from leading Aetolian families). **Attested**.
- **Wealth, patronage, and local influence** matter more than formal rank alone. **Attested**.
- **Geographic dispersion** (mountainous Aetolia + far-flung federates) limits central control and empowers local elites. **Attested**.
- **Prestige from military success** (279–278 Gallic resistance) enhances strategos/elite standing. **Reconstructed**. Kept strictly separate from legal authority.

## J.1 Citizenship, social classes and political participation

- **Federal membership principle:** citizens of member communities are, in effect, members of the league for federal purposes (the assembly is open to all citizens of member communities). **Attested** (principle).
- **Isopolity / dual citizenship (later attested):** league documents show grants of *politeia* (citizenship) by the league to outsiders and reciprocal arrangements; the **precise extent at 278 BCE is uncertain**. **Uncertain / gap**.
- **Participation:** direct assembly in principle, but practical attendance limited by distance, seasonality (two regular meetings), cost — favors wealthier elites (the *Panaetolicum* assembly often interpreted as an assembly of influential persons in practice). **Reconstructed**.
- **Classes:** free male citizens of member communities; non-citizen residents (metics/aliens); slaves/unfree. Aristocratic/elite families prominent. **Attested** (general).
- **Eligibility for federal office:** member-community citizen (Aetolian/league citizen by virtue of community membership). **Reconstructed**.
- **Key point (RV3 test):** membership is **scoped to communities + federal** (two levels possible). Not a single imperial citizenship flag. **Confidence: Reconstructed**.

## N. Anachronism checks (Aetolian League, 278 BCE)

- **Do not back-project the fully developed late-3rd century federal bureaucracy** (7 tamiai, 7 epilektarchoi, larger boularchoi board, Amphictyonic dominance) onto 278 BCE without marking uncertainty. **Gap** where not attested.
- **Do not assume a fixed 4-year interval rule for strategos re-election** at 278 (later attested). **Uncertain / gap**.
- **Do not assume Roman-style cursus honorum, secret ballot, or *imperium* vocabulary.** Absent.
- **Do not treat member communities as “subjects”**; they are **federates/members** with substantial autonomy (hegemonic-indirect). **Terminology guard.**
- **Do not conflate “league citizenship grants (*politeia*)” as universal/fully developed at 278** — evidence later. **Uncertain / gap**.
- **Gallic campaign context (279–278):** aftermath, not yet the full imperial phase of the league. Keep date-drift explicit.
- **No “Aetolia as a nation-state”**; it’s a **confederation/koinon** (F3). Avoid anachronistic nation-state language.

## Taxonomy test summary (F1–F7, role kinds, RV1–RV4)

- **Family:** **F3 — Federal league / *koinon***. **No combination required** at 278 (contrast Epirus = F3+F2). Fits cleanly.
- **Seven role kinds:** all present as needed (Political office, Military command, Administrative appointment; Religious office appears limited/uncertain at 278 — **gap**, not a contradiction). No new role kind required.
- **RV1 (indirect influence/hegemony):** **Strongly supported.** Member communities = hegemonic-indirect (alliance/federation without direct administration). Fits taxonomy §6.1 exactly.
- **RV2 (obligation/privilege vocabulary):** **Supported (testable).** Military levy + alignment obligations vs. internal autonomy/own magistrates privileges — matches §6.2. Evidence at 278 partial.
- **RV3 (tiered/grantable/local membership):** **Supported as proposal.** Two-level (community + league) membership fits scoped/tiered model; grantable *politeia* attested later (uncertain at 278). Architecture can represent without Rome-shaped flag.
- **RV4 (four dimensions separate):** **Strongly supported.** Legal authority (corporate federal bodies), political influence (member autonomy/compliance varies), geographic control (decentralised), economic obligations (contributions/booty) are distinct. Mirrors ADR-0020 separation.
- **Verdict:** Taxonomy F1–F7 + RV1–RV4 are **sufficient** to represent the Aetolian League at 278 BCE **without forcing Mediterranean terminology**. The principal challenges are **evidence gaps (uncertainty)** and the need to model **corporate grant bodies (N1)** and **nested membership (N5)** — both already proposed (N/RV). No genuinely new architectural family/role-kind is required by this sheet.
