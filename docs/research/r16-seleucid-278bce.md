## A. Record header

- **Polity:** the Seleucid Empire (the *basileia* of the Seleucids).
- **Date of record:** 278 BCE — **Antiochus I Soter** (co-regent from ~292/291;
  sole *basileus* from 281 after Seleucus I Nicator's assassination). The Gallic
  invasion of Anatolia (278/277) is **in progress**; his victory over the
  Galatians (the "Elephant Battle") is **still in the future** (~275/274).
- **Government summary:** a **personal conquest-monarchy** in which the king
  *is* the state; authority flows from the crown through **court "Friends"**
  (*philoi*) and **delegated governors** (satraps/*strategoi*) down to **subject
  peoples**, **Greek *poleis***, and **native temple estates**. No legal check
  on the king exists; only practical ones (army, court factions, powerful
  subordinates, civic and priestly bodies).
- **Scope at the date:** from the Aegean coast of Anatolia to the Iranian
  plateau and **Bactria/Sogdiana** — the largest of the Successor states; the
  **upper satrapies** (Media, Persis, Parthia, Bactria, Aria, Arachosia,
  Drangiana, Carmania, Hyrcania) and the western centres (Antioch, Seleucia-
  on-the-Tigris). Control of western Anatolia is **contested** with Ptolemaic
  Egypt and local dynasts.
- **Evidence base:** literary (Appian *Syr.*, Diodorus, Justin, Polybius,
  Strabo, Plutarch), **Babylonian cuneiform** (the Astronomical Diaries,
  Seleucid-era chronicles), **Greek inscriptions** (civic decrees, royal
  letters), **coins** (royal portrait types, mints). **Date-drift warning:** most
  detail on Seleucid administration is 2nd-century-BCE (Polybius, and the later
  "seleucid administrative" picture); the 3rd-century court is more thinly
  attested.
- **Sheet author / date:** R-16 research, 2026-10-14.

## D. Selection mechanisms

- **Kingship:** hereditary in the Seleucid house (with a mythic descent from
  **Apollo** used for legitimacy); in practice **acclamation by the army** and
  the absence of rivals mattered as much as birth. Co-regency was used to
  secure succession (Antiochus I was co-regent from ~292/291).
- **Governors and court:** **royal appointment**, revocable; the crown is the
  sole grantor.
- **Cities:** internal offices chosen by **local civic procedures** (assemblies,
  councils), subject to royal confirmation and the king's *charter*.
- **Priesthoods:** **hereditary** within families / temple corporations.

## E. Tenure, terms and re-entry

- **King:** life; ends by death, deposition, or defeat.
- **Governor/court appointment:** **no fixed term**; ends by revocation, death,
  or reassignment — *indefinite, at the king's pleasure* (a primitive the
  architecture must express: §M/C2).
- **Civic offices:** usually **annual**, per the city's own law.
- No universal term limits or age ladders.

## F. Legal powers (capabilities)

- **King:** the full set — `declare_war`, `ratify_treaty`, `levy_tax`,
  `authorise_spending(scope, limit)`, `appoint_office(role)`, `command_army`,
  `conduct_ritual` (sacral kingship), `propose_law`/legislate-by-edict, supreme
  `judge`. The king is the **sovereignty root** (taxonomy §4).
- **Governor:** a **delegated subset** bounded by the appointment — tax, raise
  local forces, judge — with no independent sovereignty.
- **City:** internal `propose_law`/`convene_assembly` **within its charter**;
  no war/peace.
- **Priest:** `conduct_ritual`; estate authority over temple land.

## G. Institutional checks

- **None legal on the king.** Checks are **practical**: army loyalty, court
  factions, the standing of great subordinates (satraps, dynasts), the autonomy
  claims of Greek cities, the influence of native priesthoods, and rival
  dynastic claimants (e.g. the Ptolemaic claim to Syria). Model these as
  **influence/coercion inputs**, never as a veto capability (AD-25 §4.1).

## H. Military command authority

- The king is commander-in-chief; **command is normally delegated** to
  *strategoi*, *philoi*, and allied dynasts. Core forces: the **Macedonian-style
  phalanx** (*argyraspides*, "Silver Shields"), **Companion cavalry**
  (*hetairoi*), mercenaries (including, from 278/277, **Galatian** bands),
  and **war elephants** (the Seleucid emblem from the Indian expedition of
  Seleucus I). At 278 the army is engaged against the **Galatian** incursion in
  Anatolia. Command is fused with delegated royal authority, not a separate
  civic *imperium*.

## I. Financial authority

- **"Treasury = state" is unsafe.** There is a **royal** fisc, but also
  **city** revenues, **temple** estates, and **royal land** (*chora basilike*)
  worked by subject populations and allotments (*kleroi*) to colonists/
  soldiers. Revenue: land tax, tribute from subject peoples, city taxes,
  harbour/road dues, mines, and the great agricultural surplus of Mesopotamia
  and Syria. **Spending authority is the king's**, exercised through appointed
  stewards. This feeds AD-26/R-15 (area 7).

## J. Informal political influence

- Court proximity to the king (the whole point of *philoi* ranks); **kinship**
  with the royal house; **military reputation**; **wealth** and landed estates;
  **ethnic networks** (Greco-Macedonian *versus* native elites); **priestly**
  standing; **city patronage**. Kept strictly separate from legal authority
  (AD-25 §4.1).

## J.1 Citizenship, social classes and political participation

- **No single "Seleucid citizenship."** The political community is layered:
  **Greco-Macedonian** settlers (the *politeuma* of the army and the cities),
  **Greek *polis* citizens** (who hold civic rights locally), **native
  populations** (Babylonians, Syrians, Iranians — subjects, not citizens of a
  king-state), **temple dependents**, **royal-land cultivators** (the *laoi*,
  attested mainly later), and **slaves**. "Citizenship" is **local (of a
  *polis*)**, not imperial; imperial belonging is **subjection**, expressed
  through tribute and obedience, not franchise. This is the key eligibility
  substrate AD-25 needs: there is **no imperial electorate**.

## J.2 Relationships with subordinate communities

- **Satrapies/provinces** (governed by appointed satraps/*strategoi*): tribute,
  troops, administration.
- **Greek *poleis*** (Antioch, Seleucia-Pieria, Apamea, Laodicea, the old Ionian
  cities…): internal self-government, civic cult, gymnasium; obligations of
  taxation and loyalty; **not** the central government.
- **Native temple estates** (Babylonian sanctuaries; Iranian cult centres):
  land, dues, local justice; the king styles himself their protector/benefactor.
- **Tribal/subject peoples** (Iranians, Anatolians): tribute, levies, garrisons.
- **Vassal dynasts** (emerging: Cappadocia, Armenia, Atropatene, Persis):
  tribute and military service under loose royal suzerainty.
- This is exactly the "a polity may contain institution types that are not its
  central government" rule (AD-25 §6.3) — the Seleucid realm is the strongest
  278 example.

## K. Formal vs. practical power (the honesty table)

| Office / body | Formal power (rules) | Practical power (in operation) | Where the gap comes from | Confidence |

| King | Absolute | Constrained by army, court, satraps, cities, cult | Distance, delegation, elite bargaining | Attested |
| Governor (satrap) | Delegated, revocable | Can become semi-autonomous (a nascent dynast) where the king is weak | Weak communications; local power bases | Attested |
| *Philoi* | Advisory only | Decisive in access and patronage; fill key posts | Court proximity = real power | Attested |
| Greek *polis* | Charters of autonomy | Real self-government, but taxed and garrisoned as needed | Royal need for revenue/troops vs. city liberties | Attested |
| Temple estate | Cult + dues | Economically and socially dominant locally; a bargaining bloc | Land + population + sacral legitimacy | Attested |

**Fit verdicts:** king = **clean** (a sovereign-root role) · governor/court
appointment = **clean-with-gloss** (needs revocable/indefinite tenure) · *polis*
= **clean** (local government; subordinate tier) · temple estate = **strained**
(a corporate institution holding land+people — needs AD-26 + institutional
membership).

## N. Anachronism checks (Seleucid, 278 BCE)

- **No formalised aulic title hierarchy.** The graded *philoi* system
  (*syngeneis, protoi philoi*, etc.) is **2nd-century-BCE** evidence (court of
  later Seleucids); at 278 the ranks are loose.
- **No Roman-style provinces or *imperium*.** Use satrapies/*strategiai*.
- **Parthia and Bactria are satrapies, not kingdoms/empires** (Arsacid revolt
  ~247; Greco-Bactrian kingdom ~256/250 — both later).
- **No "Seleucid Empire" as a bureaucratic nation-state**; it is a
  **personal monarchy over subject peoples**.
- **The Galatian settlement is not yet done** (migration 278/277); do not place
  settled Galatian *tribes* in fixed territories at 278.
- **No Roman *provincia*, no denarius, no imperial cult of the ruler at 278**
  (Seleucid ruler-cult is civic/selective, not a state church).
