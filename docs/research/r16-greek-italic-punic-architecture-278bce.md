## A. Greek *poleis* and colonial settlements (incl. Neapolis)

### A.1 Form

- **Planned colonies use the Hippodamian grid** — orthogonal streets, defined
  blocks, sometimes zoned (sanctuary/civic/residential). Colonial foundations and
  refoundations at 278 include planned cities; **older *poleis* are more
  organic**. [D/R]
- **Neapolis**: Greek colonial grid and defensive remains are attested; the city
  passed to Oscan/Samnitic control (late 5th c.) and was a Roman ally by 278.
  Its fabric at 278 is **Greek-with-Italic layer**. The exact grid plots, harbour
  basin and sanctuary footprints are **not** surveyed — omit invented piers (VS-5
  §Phase 3). [D for grid/walls; R/S for detail]

### A.2 Structures and materials

| Element | 278 BCE form | Class |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Temple | **Peripteral**, Doric/Ionic (Corinthian increasingly Hellenistic); stone platform, colonnade, gabled roof, terracotta tile; marble in wealthier/Hellenistic contexts | [D] |
| Agora | Open civic/market space, often framed by **stoas** (colonnaded halls) | [D] |
| Stoa | Long colonnaded hall — trade, civic, religious uses | [D] |
| Theatre | **Semicircular, slope-built** (Greek type); not Roman arch-stage | [D] |
| Bouleuterion | Council-house, often rectilinear with seating | [D/R] |
| Gymnasium / palaestra | Athletic/civic complexes around a court | [D/R] |
| Walls | Ashlar or **mud-brick on a stone socle**; towers; **bent/offset gateways**; artillery-ready features | [D] |
| Houses | Courtyard/**pastas** houses; **peristyle** houses emerging in the Hellenistic period | [R] |
| Fortifications | Towers increasingly common (4th c. onward) | [D] |
| Harbour | Quays, **moles** (breakwaters), enclosed basins where geography allows | [D/R] |

### A.3 Distinguishing cues

Colonnaded frames; ordered stone temples; grid streets in planned cities; theatre
cut into a slope; absence of Roman imperial forms.

### A.4 Anachronism guards

- **No** Roman arch/amphitheatre forms; **no** Roman-imperial temples.
- The **Great Altar of Pergamon** and high Pergamene gigantomachy monuments are
  ~2nd c. BCE — not 278.
- Fully developed **colonnaded streets** as a default are later; use them
  sparingly and as [R].

## B. Samnite and other Italic fortified hill settlements

### B.1 Form

- **Hilltop, terraced, agglomerative** layouts — rarely gridded; the circuit
  follows the plateau edge. [D/R]
- Samnites are Roman *socii* by 278; the settlement tradition (fortified
  hill-towns, sanctuaries) continues. [D]

### B.2 Structures and materials

| Element | 278 BCE form | Class |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Walls | **Polygonal/"cyclopean"** limestone masonry (large irregular polygonal blocks); hillfort circuits; simple gates | [D] |
| Sanctuary | Italic podium temple; open cult areas; terraced sanctuaries (monumental examples like Pietrabbondante are later — careful) | [R] |
| Houses | Smaller clustered dwellings, terrain-following; stone/timber; tile or thatch | [R] |
| Assembly | Open meeting area / plateau space | [S] |
| Infrastructure | Cisterns, terracing; limited paving | [R] |

### B.3 Distinguishing cues

Earth-toned hilltop silhouette; **polygonal walls**; terraces; lack of regular
grid; absence of monumental colonial architecture. (VS-5's Norba sample is the
in-repo precedent; note the wall-phase dating caution.)

### B.4 Anachronism guards

- **No** Roman colonial orthogonal plan or theatre/amphitheatre.
- **No** later monumental Italic sanctuaries as a default (2nd c. BCE).

## C. Carthaginian / Punic coastal settlements

### C.1 Form

- **Harbour-led coastal layout**; at Carthage the **Byrsa** acropolis above twin
  harbour basins. [R — much is described in later sources and heavily destroyed
  in 146 BCE, then rebuilt Roman]
- Punic **emporia** and coastal towns across the western Mediterranean share
  maritime/commercial character. [D/R]

### C.2 Structures and materials

| Element | 278 BCE form | Class |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Harbours | Artificial **cothon** (enclosed basin, often with an inner naval harbour and admiralty island) — the canonical form is later-3rd/2nd c. [R] | [R] |
| Walls | Massive ashlar walls; the famous **triple land wall** is a later description [R] | [R] |
| Temple | Rectangular temple (e.g. to Tanit/Baal Hammon); **open-air tophet** sanctuary with rows of stelae | [D] |
| Houses | Courtyard houses with **cisterns** and plaster; some with *signinum*-type floors | [D/R] |
| Commercial | Warehouses, quays, shipping halls | [R] |
| Materials | Local **sandstone**, plaster, mud brick; ashlar masonry | [R] |

### C.3 Distinguishing cues

Artificial harbour basins and moles; tophet stelae precinct; merchant-city
density; sandstone/plaster palette.

### C.4 Anachronism guards

- **No** Roman Carthage (post-146 BCE monumental city).
- The canonical **cothon** and **triple-wall** forms are later descriptions —
  label [R]; do not render them as 278 fact.
- **No** medieval or Islamic-period forms.

## D. Hellenistic royal and administrative centres (incl. Alexandria)

### D.1 Form

- **Large planned grids** with processional axes; a walled enceinte, a citadel or
  palace quarter, and monumental sanctuaries. Alexandria is the type: grid plan,
  the **Canopic Way**, the **Basileia** (palace quarter), the Great Harbour,
  canals. [D/R]
- Royal cities of the Seleucid realm (Antioch, Seleucia-on-the-Tigris,
  Dura-Europos-type foundations) share grid planning and palatial/administrative
  architecture. [D/R]

### D.2 Structures and materials

| Element | 278 BCE form | Class |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palace quarter | Royal residence + administration terraces | [D/R] |
| Mouseion / library | Alexandria's Mouseion/Library (Ptolemy I/II, c. 285) | [D for existence; R for form] |
| Lighthouse | **Pharos of Alexandria** — completed c. 280 BCE | [D/R] |
| Temple | Monumental Greek + **Serapeum**-type; and **Egyptian** temple forms coexist (hybrid) | [D/R] |
| Colonnade | Porticoes and peristyle courts; **street colonnades are developing, not yet the Roman norm** | [R] |
| Houses | Peristyle houses for elite; dense ordinary housing | [R] |
| Fortifications | Strong enceinte; harbour fortification | [R] |
| Materials | Ashlar, marble, terracotta; monumental scale | [D] |

### D.3 Distinguishing cues

Grid + processional avenue; palace/citadel; harbour + lighthouse; hybrid
Greek/Egyptian religious architecture; monumental scale unmatched at 278 outside
the Hellenistic world.

### D.4 Anachronism guards

- **Pharos** is ~just completed (~280) — [D/R], do not over-detail.
- **No** Roman-imperial imperial forms, colonnaded streets as a late-Roman norm,
  or Late Antique forms.
- Pergamon's great monuments are ~2nd c. — not part of the 278 royal-centre look.

## E. Additional traditions (only where evidence supports distinction)

| Tradition | 278 BCE architectural character | Class |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Etruscan** | Tufa cities, sometimes gridded (Marzabotto); ashlar tufa walls with **arch gates**; deep-porch **podium temples** with terracotta revetment; atrium-like courtyard houses | [D/R] |
| **Egyptian (Ptolemaic, non-Alexandria)** | Mud-brick towns along the Nile; monumental **pylon** temples and hypostyle halls — **many completed after 278** (see register); flat-roof houses; palm | [D/R] |
| **Mesopotamian / Babylonian** | Mud-brick city mounds; temples/palaces; ancient ziggurats as standing ruins; flat roofs; bitumen; glazed brick is earlier | [D/R] |
| **Iranian (Seleucid / sub-kingdom)** | Royal/administrative centres; columned audiences halls; Achaemenid-Hellenistic fusion; terraced sites | [R] |
| **Gallic / Celtic** | Open and hilltop settlements; **timber/earth ramparts** and ditches (**murus gallicus** and major *oppida* are later — guard); **wattle-and-daub** round/rectilinear houses; thatch | [D/R] |
| **Iberian / Celtiberian** | Hilltop **oppida** with stone/rubble walls and towers; rectilinear stone/earth houses; some coherent blocks | [D/R] |
| **Thracian / Geto-Dacian** | Fortresses/settlements; stone-timber citadels and earthworks; Hellenistic borrowings | [R] |
| **South-Arabian** | Oasis/incense-route towns; stone/rubble walls and towers; **multi-storey tower houses**; temples; dam/canal irrigation (Ma'rib-type) | [D/R] |
| **Mauryan (eastern edge)** | Timber-rich palace/city centres; earth/timber ramparts and moats; **no Ashokan pillars (post-268)**, no monumental Sanchi-type stupas, no NBPW | [D/R] |

## F. Cross-tradition comparison (compact)

| Axis | Greek/Hellenistic | Italic hill | Punic | Egyptian | Celtic/Iberian |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plan | Grid (planned) / organic | Organic hilltop | Harbour-led | Nile-tied | Hilltop / open |
| Wall | Ashlar/mud-brick + towers | Polygonal stone | Massive ashlar | Mud brick/stone | Earth-timber / rubble stone |
| Signature | Colonnade, order, theatre | Polygonal wall, terrace | Cothon, tophet | Pylon, flat roof | Round house, rampart |
| Roof | Gable, tile | Tile/thatch | Tile/flat | **Flat** | Thatch |
| Monumentality at 278 | High (Hellenistic) | Low–moderate | Moderate | High (temple) | Low |

## G. Evidence and uncertainty

- **Directly supported:** Greek grid plans and orders; Italic polygonal walls;
  Punic tophet; Egyptian pylons (as a class); Alexandrian grid and Pharos
  existence.
- **Reconstructed:** Punic harbour/wall detail; Neapolis detail; Pergamon at 278;
  Egyptian temple completion dates.
- **Speculative:** colours, roof hues, street furniture, unexcavated sanctuaries,
  timber superstructures.
- **Guard:** do not flatten multi-phase sites to 278. See the evidence register.
