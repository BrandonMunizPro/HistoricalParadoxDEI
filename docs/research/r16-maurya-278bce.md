# R-16 research sheet — Maurya Empire at 278 BCE

**Status: Proposal (2026-10-14), part of R-16.** Research evidence only; all findings are **Proposal**. AD-25 remains **OPEN**. ## A. Record header

- **Polity:** the **Maurya Empire** (*Mauryavamsa*), founded by Chandragupta Maurya; at 278 BCE it is under the reign of **Bindusara** (*Amitraghāta*).
- **Date of record:** **278 BCE**. Chronology (conventional, with margin of error ±1–2 years): Chandragupta’s accession c. 322 BCE; Bindusara acc. c. 297 BCE; Ashoka acc. c. 273 BCE (with 4-year interval to consecration c. 269/268). Therefore **278 BCE = late reign of Bindusara** (c. 19 years into his reign). **Do not project Ashokan edicts/institutions (post-261 context, esp. Dhamma-mahāmātras) backward into 278 BCE.**
- **Government summary (one sentence):** a **large-scale imperial monarchy** (personal/royal monarchy) with a centralized bureaucratic apparatus, provincial administration (including viceroys/princes in key provinces), a standing army, and a complex revenue system; territorial extent included much of the subcontinent (north + substantial Deccan) with varying integration of peripheral areas.
- **Territorial/structural scope at the date:** Magadha core (capital **Pāṭaliputra**), provinces (Taxila/Uttarapatha, Ujjayini/Avanti region, possibly Tosali/Kalinga integration stage varies, and southern provinces toward Karnataka region) — precise provincial map at 278 BCE **uncertain**. Peripheral areas show differing degrees of control (direct administration vs. tributary/indirect).
- **Evidence base:** indigenous textual (*Arthashastra* — composite text with layers; *Purāṇas*; Buddhist *Dīpavaṃsa/Mahāvaṃsa* later; *Aśokāvadāna* later), foreign (Megasthenes’ *Indica* fragments c. 302 BCE under Chandragupta; Deimachos under Bindusara — very fragmentary), epigraphy (Ashokan edicts post-257 BCE; no extant Bindusara inscriptions). **Date-drift warning:** *Arthashastra* ideal-type; later traditions often retroject Ashokan practices. Treat as **Reconstructed/Uncertain** where pre-Ashokan attestation lacking.
- **Sheet author/date:** R-16 research, 2026-10-14.

### C.1 Notes on offices at 278 BCE

- **Bindusara context:** foreign sources (Deimachos fragments) confirm Mauryan court/embassies; internal office structure for Bindusara not directly attested. The *Tīrthas/Adhyakṣas* scheme comes from *Arthashastra* (composite). **Treat as ideal-type; real structure at 278 BCE may be simpler/variant.**
- **Tenure:** “at the king’s discretion” matches **revocable/indefinite tenure (C2)**; explicitly fits monarchy with sovereign root (C1). **Confidence: Reconstructed**.
- **Dual kinds:** king combines political + military + religious — normal for F2.

## D. Selection mechanisms

- **Hereditary succession (kingship):** Maurya dynasty. **Attested**.
- **Royal appointment (all other offices):** king appoints ministers, viceroys, *Tīrthas*, *Adhyakṣas*, *Amātyas*. **Reconstructed**.
- **Tests/selection criteria (*Arthashastra*):** recommends rigorous tests for *Amātyas* (character, competence). **Textual ideal-type** — direct evidence for systematic testing at 278 BCE **Uncertain / gap**.
- **Provincial viceroys:** often royal princes (*Kumārā*) appointed by king (especially key provinces); non-royal governors also possible. **Reconstructed**.
- **Local intermediaries:** *Gopas/Grāmaṇīs* may involve local selection? *Arthashastra* implies appointment/oversight; direct evidence **Uncertain / gap**.

## E. Tenure, terms and re-entry

- **King:** life tenure. **Attested**.
- **All appointed officials:** **indefinite tenure, held at the king’s pleasure (revocable)**. No fixed terms attested for Bindusara period. **Reconstructed** — matches C2.
- **Reappointment/removal:** at royal discretion. **Reconstructed**.
- **No electoral term limits** (monarchical). **Attested by form**.

**Corporate grant (N1):** unlike the Aetolian assembly, grantor is **the individual sovereign** (king), not a corporate body. This is the monarchic contrast.

## G. Institutional checks

- **Advisory only:** *Mantriparishad* advises but has no constitutional veto over king. **Reconstructed**.
- **Espionage/oversight:** *Cara/Puruṣa* network provides intelligence on officials/population (a form of internal oversight). **Reconstructed** (*Arthashastra*).
- **Bureaucratic hierarchy:** officials report up the chain; Samāharta/Sannidhāta provide fiscal checks/accountability within system. **Reconstructed**.
- **Pragmatic constraints:** elite support (ministers, army commanders), succession dynamics, provincial loyalty — **informal/pragmatic**, not institutional veto. **Reconstructed**.
- **No popular assembly with sovereign competence** attested. **Attested by absence**.

## H. Military command authority

- **King is supreme commander.** **Attested**.
- **Standing army:** large standing force (infantry, cavalry, elephants, chariots, navy elements) under centralized command. **Reconstructed** (Megasthenes under Chandragupta; structure under Bindusara inferred).
- **Provincial contingents:** provinces contribute troops/support under viceroys/princes but under royal command in campaigns. **Reconstructed**.
- **Senāpati** executes command as deputy. **Reconstructed**.
- **Logistics centralized** (supply, arsenals, routes). **Reconstructed** (*Arthashastra*).

## I. Financial authority

- **Royal treasury (*Kośa*)** distinct from personal wealth? *Arthashastra* treats state treasury as royal/state resource. **Reconstructed**.
- **Revenue sources:** land tax (principal), trade/customs, mines, forests, tribute from vassals/tributary areas, fines. **Reconstructed**.
- **Assessment/collection:** **Samāharta** oversees assessment; **Sannidhāta** custodian; provincial/district officials collect. **Reconstructed**.
- **Expenditure:** authorized by king/delegates; strict oversight of accounts (*Arthashastra*). **Reconstructed**.
- **Provincial finances:** viceroys administer provincial revenue but remit/allocate per royal policy. **Reconstructed**.

## J. Informal political influence

- **Court factions** (ministers, purohita, royal family, army commanders). **Reconstructed**.
- **Brahmanical influence** (purohita, ritual legitimacy). **Reconstructed**.
- **Wealthy families/merchants** influence via court access? Evidence limited at 278 BCE. **Uncertain / gap**.
- **Provincial elites** (local rulers, headmen) mediate between center and population. **Reconstructed**.
- **Dynastic prestige** (Maurya founder). **Reconstructed**. Kept strictly separate from legal authority (king’s authority is legal/royal, not merely informal).

## J.1 Citizenship, political membership, social classes

- **No imperial “citizenship” in Greek/Roman sense.** Political membership is not framed as an electorate. Authority flows from king to subjects/officials. **Attested by form**.
- **Varṇa/jāti context:** social stratification present; eligibility for high offices often favors certain groups (Brahmins for purohita, ksatriya/royal for princes; *Amātya* recruitment more merit-oriented in *Arthashastra* ideal). **Reconstructed**.
- **Subjects vs. members:** vast majority are **subjects** of the empire (not “citizens” with voting rights). Provincial populations owe allegiance/tribute; political participation minimal/none at imperial level. **Reconstructed**.
- **Local communities:** villages (*grāma*), towns (*nagara*), guilds (*śreṇī*) have internal organization but limited autonomy (overseen by officials). **Reconstructed**.
- **Key point (RV3 test):** **tiered membership absent** in the Mediterranean electoral sense. The model must represent **subjects under royal authority** without imposing a citizen flag. This is a genuine test: membership is primarily **subjection/subjecthood**, not graded political franchise. **Confidence: Reconstructed**.

## J.2 Relationships with subordinate communities

| Tier / relationship | Nature | Obligations | Privileges / autonomy | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Core provinces** | Directly administered by royal viceroys/princes + imperial officials | Land tax, troops/support, obedience to royal orders | Internal administration under imperial oversight; limited local autonomy | **Reconstructed** |
| **Conquered territories integrated** | Direct administration (e.g. parts of Deccan integrated under Bindusara) | Tribute/tax, military contributions | Varies; often replaced/overlaid local rulers with imperial officials | **Reconstructed** |
| **Vassal/tributary rulers** | Local rulers who accept Mauryan suzerainty | Tribute, military aid when required, alignment | Retain internal rule, titles, customs (indirect control) | **Reconstructed** |
| **Tribal/forest communities** | Peripheral groups | Often tribute in kind or symbolic; less regular taxation | Substantial autonomy in internal affairs; minimal direct administration | **Reconstructed** (*Arthashastra* mentions *āṭavika* etc.) |
| **Trade/urban centers** | Administered towns with guilds | Taxes/customs, regulations | Guild autonomy in internal economic matters (regulated) | **Reconstructed** (Megasthenes) |
| **Religious institutions/temple estates?** | Not prominent in Mauryan sources as autonomous tiers like Egyptian temples; state regulation emphasized | Unclear at 278 BCE | Limited attestation | **Uncertain / gap** |

**Administration mode (RV1):** **spectrum** — core = **direct administration**; peripheral tributaries = **hegemonic-indirect** (suzerainty without full direct rule); tribal = often indirect. This fits taxonomy §6.1 (direct/hegemonic/corporate). **No clear *polis*-style corporate containment analogous to Greek poleis** attested as a major tier.

## N. Anachronism checks (Maurya, 278 BCE)

- **Do not project Ashokan edicts/institutions backward.** *Dhamma-mahāmātras* first attested in Ashokan MRE V (year 13, post-Kalinga c. 256 BCE context) — **absent at 278 BCE**. Do not assume Ashokan moral administration.
- **Do not treat *Arthashastra* as a literal Mauryan handbook for 278 BCE.** Composite text; use as ideal-type, mark uncertainty. **Date-drift guard.**
- **Do not assume Greek/Roman citizenship or electoral institutions.** **Inappropriate terminology.** Use “subjects”, “tributaries”, “vassals”, “officials”.
- **Do not assume a uniform “empire” in modern sense with full penetration.** Spectrum of control (direct/indirect). **Gap** on precise provincial boundaries at 278 BCE.
- **Do not retroject Ashokan provincial capitals/structure rigidly.** Provincial organization evolved; Bindusara’s provincial map uncertain.
- **Do not assume land grants as systematic at 278 BCE** (attested later in some forms). **Uncertain / gap**.
- **Chronology guard:** 278 BCE = **Bindusara reign** (pre-Ashokan). Explicitly state this throughout mapping.

## Taxonomy test summary (F1–F7, role kinds, RV1–RV4)

- **Family:** **F2 — Royal (personal) monarchy**. Fits core (sovereign king, court/officials, army). No combination required at imperial level; peripheral tiers don’t create a new family. **Consistent**.
- **Seven role kinds:** **Political office, Military command, Religious office, Administrative appointment** are core. **Social rank** present (varṇa/jāti). **Property/landholding** appears contextually (treasury/land grants uncertain). **Institutional membership** appears for advisory bodies/guilds (corporate) but not as sovereign electorate. No new role kind required.
- **RV1 (indirect influence/hegemony):** **Strongly supported.** Tributary rulers/tribal areas = hegemonic-indirect (suzerainty without full direct administration). Core provinces = direct. Spectrum matches §6.1.
- **RV2 (obligation/privilege vocabulary):** **Supported.** Land tax, tribute, military contributions as obligations; retained internal rule for tributaries as privilege. Must be recorded separately from rights.
- **RV3 (tiered/grantable/local membership):** **Test case shows non-electoral model is needed.** Subjecthood (minimal political rights, obligations to crown) is the dominant form. Architecture must represent **tiered subjection** without forcing Roman-style citizenship/franchise. This is a **load-bearing test** — confirms RV3 must allow subject tiers.
- **RV4 (four dimensions separate):** **Strongly supported.** Legal authority (king+delegates) vs. political influence (court factions, provincial elites) vs. geographic control (distance-dependent, varies by region) vs. economic obligations (tax/tribute flows) — must never collapse. Mirrors ADR-0020.
- **Verdict:** Taxonomy F1–F7 + RV1–RV4 are **sufficient** to represent the Maurya Empire at 278 BCE **without forcing Mediterranean terminology**. Key requirements are **C1 (sovereign root)** and **C2 (revocable tenure)** — already in proposals. The model must accommodate **non-electoral membership (subjecthood)** and **distance-dependent control**. No genuinely new architectural family/role-kind is required. The main limitation is **evidence uncertainty** due to chronological gap (Bindusara vs. Ashokan sources).
