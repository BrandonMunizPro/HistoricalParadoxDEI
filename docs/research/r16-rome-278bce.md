## A. Record header

- **Polity:** the Roman Republic (res publica).
- **Date of record:** 278 BCE (middle of the Pyrrhic War; Romans defeated at
  Asculum 279, refused peace, campaigned on).
- **Government summary:** an **oligarchic republic** whose *form* is annually
  elected, collegiate magistrates checked by mutual veto and tribunician
  intercession, sovereign citizen assemblies, and a customary (unwritten)
  *mos maiorum*; whose *practice* is dominated by a small aristocratic elite
  (the *nobiles*) acting through a formally advisory Senate.
- **Scope at the date:** peninsular Italy south of the Po, an alliance system of
  Latin and Italian *socii*; **no overseas provinces yet** (*provincia* = a
  sphere of duty, not a territory).
- **Political community at the date:** a **ladder of statuses**, not one
  citizenship — Roman citizens (patrician and plebeian), *cives sine suffragio*,
  Latins (Latin rights), and treaty-bound Italian *socii*, above slaves and
  foreigners. See **§J.1** (membership) and **§J.2** (how the *socii* system
  actually worked).
- **Evidence base:** primary literary (Polybius, Livy, Plutarch, Cicero,
  Diodorus); the consular *Fasti*; modern scholarship. **Date-drift warning:**
  Polybius' account of the Roman "mixed constitution" describes the **mid-2nd
  century BCE**, and Livy's later books describe later arrangements; both must be
  back-dated with care.
- **Sheet author / date:** R-16 research, 2026-10-14.

## D. Selection mechanisms

- **Election by bloc assembly** is the norm: consuls/praetors/censors by
  *centuriata*; curule aediles/quaestors by *tributa*; tribunes/plebeian aediles
  by *concilium plebis*. Voting is **by bloc** (century or tribe), each bloc
  casting one vote, often in a fixed order that favours the wealthiest.
- **Voting is open** at 278 (voice/division). **Anachronism risk:** the secret
  ballot (*lex Gabinia tabellaria* and successors, from 139 BCE) does **not**
  exist yet.
- **Senate enrolment** is by the **censors** (*lectio senatus*), drawing on the
  ex-magistrate pool. Whether the quaestorship *automatically* conferred Senate
  membership in the 3rd century is **contested** (a minority view holds it did;
  the mainstream places automatic enrolment with Sulla). Treat as reconstructed.
- **Priestly colleges** recruit by co-option (historically); the *lex Ogulnia*
  (300) opened the major colleges to plebeians.
- **Dictator**: appointed, not elected, on the Senate's advice.
- **Usurpation** is not a legitimate route and must be modelled as an
  *illegal* action outside the gate (architecture §4.1).

## E. Tenure, terms and re-entry

- **Annual** for consul, praetor, quaestor, aedile, tribune.
- **Censor:** elected every ~5 years, serving **18 months**.
- **Dictator:** **6 months** maximum.
- **Priesthoods:** lifetime.
- **Iteration:** re-election to the consulship is **possible** at 278 (the year's
  own consuls are both holding the office a second time). A firm ten-year
  re-election interval is **not** yet a rule at 278.
- **Not attested at 278 (anachronism risks):** no minimum-age ladder and no fixed
  sequence of offices (the *lex Villia annalis*, 180 BCE, comes later); no
  codified *cursus honorum* with legal force.

## F. Legal powers (capabilities)

- **Consul:** `command_army`, `raise_forces`, `declare_war` (with the assembly),
  `convene_assembly`, `convene_senate`, `propose_law`, `veto` (over an equal),
  `ratify_treaty` (with Senate/assembly), `conduct_auspices`, limited
  `iurisdictio`.
- **Praetor:** `command_army` (if needed), `iurisdictio` (the core function),
  `convene_senate`, `convene_assembly`, `propose_law`.
- **Quaestor:** `custody_of_funds`, `disburse_funds(scope)` **only when
  authorised by the Senate** — a clean case of *corporate authorisation*
  (architecture §5).
- **Censor:** `conduct_census`, `enrol_senators`, `let_public_contracts`,
  `issue_nota`.
- **Tribune:** `veto` (intercessio) over any magistrate's act; `auxilium`;
  `convene_concilium_plebis`; `propose_plebiscite`.
- **Aedile:** `stage_games`, `regulate_markets`, `oversee_works`, some
  `iurisdictio`.
- **Dictator:** superset of consular capabilities for the term.
- **Priest:** `conduct_ritual`, `take_auspices`, `regulate_calendar`.

A capability is **permission only**. The *means* come from the treasury/census
(§I) and the *compliance* from *auctoritas*, patronage and the crowd (§J).

## G. Institutional checks

- **Collegiality:** equal co-holders may each block the other (*par potestas* /
  *intercessio*). A magistrate may also intercede against a **lower-ranked**
  magistrate.
- **Tribunician intercession:** a tribune can veto *any* magistrate or act — and
  no ordinary magistrate can veto a tribune. **Crucial for §L:** the tribune is
  *not* a magistrate (lower "rank"), yet the tribune's veto outranks every
  magistrate's. Veto strength is **not** a function of office precedence.
- **Provocatio ad populum:** a citizen's appeal against a magistrate's coercitio;
  a constraint on *imperium* **inside** Rome. *Imperium* is at full force only
  *militiae* (abroad); *domi* it is bounded.
- **Annual accountability:** magistrates may be prosecuted after their term.
- **Censorial *nota*:** censors may expel a senator (*praeteritus*) or degrade a
  citizen's status.

## H. Military command authority

- **Unified with civil office** at the top: consuls and praetors hold *imperium*
  and lead armies. This is a *contrast* with Carthage (§ separate sheet).
- **Proconsular prorogation** extends a commander's *imperium* beyond the year —
  the mechanism by which the Pyrrhic War is fought by ex-consuls as proconsuls.
- **Junior command:** military tribunes serve under a superior's *imperium*;
  quaestors supply and may assume command if the commander dies.
- **Coercion vs. authority:** a Roman commander acting against the *res publica*
  would be acting **outside** legality (architecture §4.1, N-12) — there is no
  granted capability to march on Rome.

## I. Financial authority

- **The public treasury (*aerarium*)** is held in the temple of Saturn and
  managed by **quaestors under Senate direction**; the Senate "held all
  magistrates responsible for expenditures" (formal supervision).
- **Censors** let public contracts (*vectigalia*, public works) and assess the
  census that underlies **tributum** (direct war tax on citizens) and the voting
  classes.
- **"Treasury = state" is *partly* safe here** (the *aerarium* is genuinely
  public) — but Roman elites' **private wealth** and the funds of **allied
  communities** are separate, so the multi-account view still applies.
- Revenue at the date: *ager publicus* rents, port dues, the *tributum*;
  **no denarius** (silver denarius from ~211 BCE): coinage is the *quadrigatus*
  /didrachm and bronze *aes*.

## J. Informal political influence (kept separate from legal authority)

- **The *nobiles*:** a small patrician–plebeian elite of families that had held
  the consulship — Fabii, Aemilii, Cornelii, Valerii, Sulpicii, Atilii, etc. Real
  access to office ran through this network.
- **Clientela / patronage:** *patroni* mobilise dependants' votes and service.
- **Senatorial *auctoritas*:** deference that makes a *senatus consultum* behave
  like an order without being one.
- **New men (*novi homines*):** e.g. the 278 consul **C. Fabricius Luscinus**,
  whose rise shows the elite was penetrable by proven ability (famous for
  incorruptibility) — but it remained exceptional.
- **Religion as influence:** augural/priestly prestige, control of the calendar
  and auspices.
- **Military reputation and the urban crowd** as levers on elections and policy.

## J.1 Citizenship, social classes and political participation

**Citizenship is *not* a universal binary.** At 278 Rome sits at the head of a
**ladder of statuses**, only the top of which is *civitas Romana*. The categories
below are the eligibility substrate for every Roman role.

| Status | Who | Political rights (**legal**) | Civil privileges | Obligations | Eligibility for office | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Roman citizen** (*cives Romani*) | The *populus Romanus* — patrician **and** plebeian | *Ius suffragii* (vote) in *centuriata*/*tributa*; plebeians also in *concilium plebis* | *Ius honorum*, *ius commercii*, *ius connubii*, ***provocatio***; *auxilium* of a tribune | Census registration; legionary service if *assiduus*; *tributum* when levied | **Yes** (subject to rank/wealth) | **Attested** |
| **Patrician / plebeian** | Two hereditary orders *within* the citizen body | Both vote; both may now hold office | Both hold the civil rights above | Same | Both eligible — since the *lex Ogulnia* (300) and *lex Hortensia* (287) the old bars are gone; the distinction is now **rank/ritual**, not a legal bar to office | **Attested** |
| **Freedman** (*libertus*) | Manumitted slave (and descendants) | Votes, but **only in the four urban tribes** (diluted weight) | Civil rights, but **barred from the *ius honorum*** | Client duties to the patron | **No** (first generation) | **Attested** |
| **Latin** (*nomen Latinum*) | Latin colonies and communities | **No** Roman vote; but *ius migrationis* (migrate to Rome → citizenship) and, for a town magistrate, citizenship | *Ius commercii*, *ius connubii* — but **not** *suffragium*/*honorum* | Military service (heavily); align in war | **No** (until enfranchised), though a Latin magistrate gains Roman citizenship | **Attested** |
| **Italian ally** (*socius*) | Samnites, Lucanians, Apulians, etc. — **treaty-bound** communities | **None** Roman | Own community's law and autonomy | Troops (*auxilia*) on request; no independent war/foreign policy | **No** | **Attested** |
| ***Cives sine suffragio*** | Some incorporated towns (e.g. much of Campania) | Roman **citizenship without the vote** | *Commercium*, *connubium*; liable to Roman burdens | Military service, *tributum* | **No** (no *suffragium*/*honorum*) | **Attested / Reconstructed** |
| ***Dediticii*** | Communities of unconditional surrender | **None** | None recognised | Whatever Rome imposes | **No** | **Reconstructed** |
| **Slave** (*servus*) | Property | **None** | None (no legal personality) | Labour; no regular military service | **No** | **Attested** |
| **Foreigner** (*peregrinus*) | Resident non-Romans | **None** | Own law where applicable | — | **No** | **Attested** |

**Political participation** (area 8): the *channels* are the bloc assemblies —
*comitia centuriata* (wealth-weighted), *comitia tributa* (35 tribes, rural
tribes dominant), *concilium plebis* (plebeians only, binding since 287). Two
critical facts for AD-25: **(a) voting weight is a function of class/wealth, not
one-person-one-vote**, and **(b) voting is open, not secret, at 278**. Eligibility
for the top offices is **legally** "Roman citizen," but **practically** narrowed
by the equestrian monetary qualification, *nobilitas*, and Senate standing — a
gap that must be modelled as **influence**, not law (§4.1).

**The obligations dimension** (distinct from rights): *assidui* (property-owning
citizens) owe legionary service by census class; the *capite censi* (those
counted by head) are **below** the regular legionary threshold; *socii* and
Latins owe *auxilia*; all owe *tributum* when the Senate levies it. **Civic
obligation ≠ military obligation ≠ tributary obligation**, and they fall on
different tiers.

> **Anti-assumption note.** A Roman-style *citizen assembly* is **not** the
> default elsewhere: Carthage's assembly is conditional and its subject Libyans
> are non-citizens (see its sheet §J.1); Ptolemaic Egypt has **no** imperial
> electorate at all (see its sheet §J.1). Do not generalise Rome's franchise.

## J.2 Relationships with subordinate communities

**Rome at 278 administers *very little* directly.** Its power is an **alliance
and incorporation system**, and the key distinction the model must hold is
**direct administration vs. treaty-based obligation vs. indirect influence**.

| Tier | Relationship type | How it is governed | Obligations | Privileges | How created / changed / ended | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ***Ager Romanus*** + *municipia* | **Direct incorporation** (Roman or *sine suffragio* citizenship) | Own local magistrates under Roman citizenship; ultimate appeals/authority to Rome | Legionary service, *tributum*, census | Roman law; *commercium*; autonomy of local office | Franchise grant; reorganisation of defeated territory | **Attested** |
| **Latin colonies** (*coloniae Latinae*) | **Autonomous allied town** (Latin status) | Own magistrates and law; **Latin rights** | Troops; align in war/peace | *Ius Latii*; *migratio*; town magistrates → Roman citizenship | **Founded** by Rome; could be enfranchised | **Attested** |
| **Roman (citizen) colonies** | **Direct**, small garrison colonies | Roman citizens on *ager publicus* | Legionary service | Full citizenship | Founded by decree | **Attested** |
| **Allied communities (*socii*)** | **Treaty** (*foedus*) — the backbone of Roman Italy | **Full internal self-government**; Rome does **not** govern them | **Troops (*auxilia*)** and alignment; **no fixed tribute** | Autonomy, own law and coinage(ish); the *foedus* guarantees | Created by **treaty** after war (some *iniquum*, unequal); ended by defection/annexation | **Attested** |
| ***Dediticii*** / subject peoples | **Unconditional subjection** | Whatever Rome dictates (often land confiscated to *ager publicus*) | Imposed terms | None recognised | *Deditio*, annexation | **Reconstructed** |
| **Carthage (external)** | **Treaty between states** (the 279/278 aid accord) | — | Mutual aid vs. Pyrrhus; no separate peace | Sovereign equality of parties | Diplomatic | **Attested** |

**Direct vs. indirect.** *Direct administration* covers only the Roman home
territory and colonies. Everything else runs through **treaties and goodwill**:
there is **no provincial machinery, no governor over allies, no tribute
administration**. The word *provincia* at 278 means **a magistrate's sphere of
duty**, not a governed territory (the first true overseas *provincia*, Sicily,
comes in 241). This is the single most important point: Rome's Italian power is
**hegemonic and treaty-based, not administrative**.

**Mechanisms of interaction.** *Foedus* (treaty), *deditio* (surrender),
*municipium* (incorporation), and colonial foundation are the four levers.
Relationships are **upgradable** (franchise) and **punishable** (land
confiscation, harsher treaty). The **obligation that matters is manpower**:
*socii* and Latins supplied a large share of Rome's armies in the Pyrrhic War,
which is exactly why their loyalty is the strategic question of 278–275.

> **Uncertainty.** The terms of individual *foedera* are **not** preserved; the
> *socii*/*Latini* distinction is well attested but the exact status of each
> community in 278 is **reconstructed** (see §P).

## M. Model challenges and refinement recommendations

| # | Evidence | Model strain | Candidate refinement (proposal to AD-25 only) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | A tribune (no magisterial rank) vetoes any magistrate; no magistrate may veto a tribune (**Attested**) | If `veto` were derived from office precedence, the tribune case breaks it | State explicitly that **veto is an explicitly granted capability**, and its *strength/scope* is data on the RoleDefinition, **not** a function of office rank |
| R2 | The Senate is **formally advisory** yet **practically decisive** (**Attested**) | A model that only records *formal* concurrence cannot express how Rome actually ran | Government configuration should distinguish **formal authority** from **conventional deference**, so a decision body's real weight is representable without falsifying its legal status |
| R3 | *Imperium* is a **power** attached to offices and **prorogued** separately from the office (**Attested**) | The three concepts cover holdings, but "holds *imperium*" is neither a role nor a stored score | Allow a **capability token** (e.g. `imperium`) to be carried by a holding and **prorogued**; treat prorogation as a holding change (§7), not a new role |
| R4 | The elective sequence (*cursus*) is **customary, not legal** at 278 (**Attested/Reconstructed**) | Eligibility data is legal, but who is *electable* is practical | Distinguish **legal eligibility** from **practical electability** (the latter an influence input, §4.1) |
| R5 | Voting is **open, by bloc**, and there is **no secret ballot** at 278 (**Attested**) | Selection is modelled but its *method* affects outcomes | Consider a **selection-method** attribute on a RoleDefinition (bloc/open vs. individual/secret) as content, not mechanics |
| R6 | Magistrates may hold **several roles at once** (consul + augur + ...) (**Attested**) | — | **Validation:** the model already supports this (§3); no change needed |

## N. Anachronism checks (Rome, 278 BCE)

- No **codified *cursus honorum*** or minimum-age/sequence law (*lex Villia
  annalis*, 180 BCE).
- No **secret ballot** (from 139 BCE).
- Only **one praetor** (the *praetor peregrinus* is c. 242 BCE).
- **~4 quaestors** (not the later 8/19/20).
- Senate **~300** (not 600/900).
- No **permanent provinces**; *provincia* = a sphere of duty (Sicily only 241).
- **No denarius** (from ~211 BCE); use *quadrigatus*/didrachm and *aes*.
- No Sullan/post-Sullan reforms; no imperial magistracies; no standing
  professional army (no Marian reform).
- Do not read **Polybius' 2nd-century** "mixed constitution" back to 278 without
  a date-drift note.

## P. Open gaps / `needs-research`

- Exact **number of quaestors** in 278 (most likely 4; not certain).
- The precise state of the **centuriate assembly's** century structure in 278
  (mid-3rd-century reorganisation is contested).
- Whether **quaestorian automatic Senate membership** held in the 3rd century
  (contested between Moore and the orthodox view).
- The **exact membership** of the Senate in 278 and the year's other magistrates.
- The **exact status of each Italian community** (citizen, *sine suffragio*,
  Latin, ally) in 278 — the *socii*/*Latini* division is attested, but not
  community-by-community; individual *foedera* terms are lost.
- The precise **size and incidence of the *tributum*** in 278, and the **census
  thresholds** separating *assidui* from *capite censi* (reconstructed).
