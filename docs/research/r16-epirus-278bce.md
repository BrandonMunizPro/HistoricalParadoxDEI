## A. Record header

- **Polity:** the **Epirote League** (*koinon ton Epeiroton*) — a **federal
  league** of Epirote tribes (chiefly **Molossians**, **Thesprotians**,
  **Chaonians**) — **under the kingship of the Molossian Aiakid house**, whose
  king **Pyrrhus** was its *hegemon*.
- **Date of record:** 278 BCE — **Pyrrhus** is **in Sicily** (crossed 278,
  invited by Syracuse against Carthage), where he was made **commander/king**
  against the Carthaginians; his son **Ptolemy** governed in Epirus as regent.
  This is the hinge between the Italian campaign (280–275) and the Sicilian
  venture (278–276).
- **Government summary:** a **two-layer** polity — a **federal league** with a
  **federal assembly** (*synedrion*/*ekklesia*) and **annual federal
  magistrates** (a *prostates*, a *strategos*, a *grammateus*, a *hieromnamon*),
  combined with the **tribal** organization of each *ethnos* and the Aiakid
  **kingship**, which holds the **hegemony** (*hegemonia*) of the league and its
  military command. This is the clearest 278 example of **federal-league +
  kingship** (taxonomy F3 with an F2 head).
- **Scope at the date:** Epirus (NW Greece), with influence over southern
  Illyria, Acarnania, and (intermittently) Thessaly/Macedon; the king's
  expeditionary armies operate **abroad** (Italy, Sicily).
- **Evidence base:** literary (Plutarch *Pyrrhus*, Diodorus, Justin, Polybius);
  **epigraphic** (the federal and tribal **decrees** of the Epirote/Molossian
  state — the key documents for the magistracies); coins. **Date-drift
  warning:** many federal-institution inscriptions are 3rd–2nd century; the 278
  form is reconstructed from them plus the narrative.
- **Sheet author / date:** R-16 research, 2026-10-14.

## D. Selection mechanisms

- **Kingship:** **hereditary** (Aiakid/Molossian house); ended by death/expulsion.
- **Federal magistrates:** **elected** by the federal assembly, **annually** —
  a genuine elective federal executive.
- **Hegemony:** held by the king **in conjunction with** the league (dual).
- **Tribal offices:** elected/magisterial within each tribe.
- **This dual structure — elected annual federal officers *under* a hereditary
  king** — is the distinctive selection pattern (taxonomy F3 + F2).

## E. Tenure, terms and re-entry

- **King:** life.
- **Federal magistrates:** **annual**, collegiate/plural in some cases, with
  re-election possible.
- Office-holding was a path to citizenship/honours (federal decrees grant
  citizenship and honours).
- No Roman-style ladder; no fixed cursus.

## F. Legal powers (capabilities)

- **King/hegemon:** `command_army`, `declare_war`, `ratify_treaty`, foreign
  policy, and leadership of the federal body.
- **Federal assembly:** `propose_law`/decree, `grant_citizenship` (a notable
  federal capability), honours, and ratifying league matters.
- **Federal magistrates:** executive/sacral `authorise` within their sphere,
  `conduct_ritual` (*hieromnamon*), record-keeping (*grammateus*), presiding
  (*prostates*).
- **Tribes:** local legislation and levy.

## G. Institutional checks

- The **federal assembly** and **tribal bodies** are real corporate checks:
  annual magistrates are accountable, and the king's hegemony depends on league
  acceptance. Unlike the pure monarchies, Epirus has **institutional** (not only
  practical) limits — but the king remains dominant in war. Model the league's
  concurrence as a **corporate capability**, and the annual elections as a
  **term-rotation** constraint.

## H. Military command authority

- The **king commands** (Pyrrhus abroad; son Ptolemy as regent at home).
  Expeditionary armies were **composite**: Epirote/Molossian levies, allied
  Greeks, mercenaries, Macedonian-style phalanx, **elephants**, and Italian
  allies. Federal *strategos* exists alongside the king's command.

## I. Financial authority

- Revenue: tribal/federal levies, royal/expeditionary plunder and subsidies
  (Pyrrhus' campaigns were partly funded by his allies and by Sicilian/Italian
  resources), sanctuary wealth at **Dodona**. "Treasury = state" is unsafe:
  tribal, federal and royal funds are distinct. This feeds AD-26/R-15.

## J. Informal political influence

- **Aiakid dynastic prestige** (kinship with Olympias/Alexander's line); heroic
  military reputation (Pyrrhus); tribal aristocracy; the sanctuary's authority;
  royal patronage across the league. Strictly separate from legal authority.

## J.1 Citizenship, social classes and political participation

- **Epirote citizenship** exists at two levels: **tribal** (*ethnos*)
  citizenship and **federal (league) citizenship**, with the league able to
  **grant citizenship and honours** by decree (a distinctive, attested federal
  competence). Political participation runs through the **federal assembly** and
  tribal assemblies. Classes: the **king and Aiakid nobility**; the **tribal
  aristocracy**; free **citizens**; non-citizen residents; and **slaves/unfree**.
  This two-level citizenship is exactly what AD-25 must not flatten.

## J.2 Relationships with subordinate communities

- **Tribes** (*ethnē*): the constituent communities of the league — internal
  self-government, contributions to the federal army.
- **Smaller Epirote communities** and **perioikic**/"subject" populations
  within tribal territory.
- **Allied/subject states abroad**: Acarnania, parts of Illyria, and (during
  campaigns) Sicilian and Italian cities that accepted Pyrrhus' command — a
  **garrison/alliance** tier, not annexation.
- **The sanctuary (Dodona)**: a separate sacral institution within the polity.

## N. Anachronism checks (Epirus, 278 BCE)

- **Do not place Pyrrhus personally in Epirus in 278** — he is **in Sicily**;
  Ptolemy is regent.
- **Do not read the fully-developed 2nd-century federal constitution backwards**
  as fixed at 278; the magistracies are attested but their 278 form is
  reconstructed.
- **No Roman province / *imperium* vocabulary.**
- **No "Epirus" as a unitary nation-state**; it is a **league of tribes under a
  king**.
- **Do not treat Sicilian/Italian commands as annexations**; they are
  expeditionary hegemony.
