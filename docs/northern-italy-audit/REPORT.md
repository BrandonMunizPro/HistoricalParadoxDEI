### Relationships around 278 BCE, rechecked

[Polybius 2.17–21](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Polybius/2%2A.html) distinguishes Insubres/Cenomani north of the Po, Veneti toward the Adriatic, and Ananes/Boii/Lingones south of it. Its geographic sequence supports relative association, not exact frontier vertices. His account of the Boii and Etruscans fighting Rome before the Pyrrhic wars does not imply Boii ownership of Etruria. Nor should the later 225 BCE alliances of Veneti/Cenomani and Boii/Insubres be imposed as 278 diplomacy. The elimination of the Senones before 278 prevents filling the entire eastern margin with an unchanged earlier tribal map.

[Brixia source concordance, KU Eichstätt](https://tp-online.ku.de/trefferanzeige_en.php?id=50) collates Livy 5.35 and Pliny's Cenomani attribution. [Patavium concordance](https://tp-online.ku.de/trefferanzeige_en.php?id=137) identifies the ancient Venetic settlement and the Livy 10.2 episode before 278. These geographically secure associations justify the corrections; the concordances themselves contain much later material and do not define 278 control. The direct Perseus Livy URL in the original audit could not be retrieved in this session; the concordance supplies a transparent alternative source trail.

Etruscan northern cultural survival, especially the Mantua area, must remain distinct from the sourced Etruscan polity reconstruction; neither has been repainted as unified northern sovereignty. Ligurians, Ananes, Lingones, Laevi/Libicii and Alpine communities constrain how much land can responsibly be assigned to these four large groups. [Strabo 4.6](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/e/roman/texts/strabo/4f%2A.html) distinguishes Taurini, Salassi, Lepontii and Raeti but is later evidence. Their precise 278 territories remain unresolved. No new peoples, settlements or research claims were invented to fill gaps. Existing Etruscan, Ligurian and Taurisci records remain intact.

### Visual implementation and scope

- Territorial raster: 4096×2048 across 6–14 E and 43–47 N, approximately 0.15–0.22 km cells rather than the original approximately 2 km cells. Source geometry is scan-converted directly; ownership IDs are nearest-sampled, never blurred into false intermediate owners. Derivative-sized seams remain readable across both scales. Provisional overlaps resolve to disputed ID 1 within this candidate instead of order-dependent ownership. The canonical fallback is retained outside the regional rectangle, avoiding clipped fields at its edge.
- Lake/shore geometry: northern source GSHHG rings are retriangulated and shared with a matching water mask. A render-only lake-bed clamp prevents terrain vertices from rising through those water surfaces. Fragment clipping follows the same mask, so political colors do not paint the water. Bounded quadratic corner reconstruction cuts at most 60 m along adjacent source edges and preserves long segments; this reduces sharp corners but is not higher-resolution survey data. Existing lake elevations and island-hole meshes are retained. Modern physical sources do not establish 278 shorelines. No decorative shoreline effects were added.
- Surface shading: reduce macro noise amplitudes, habitat modulation and ground-color variation while retaining source-derived relief normals and mountain/valley meshes. Political replacement remains 28%, with oblique illumination. No saturation increase or DEM relief exaggeration.
- Labels: regional overlay separates 15 px researched settlement labels and dot markers, 19 px faction text at strategic/regional scale, and subdued 22 px geographic region text. Settlement text is placed at existing record coordinates. Screen collision checks protect settlement text; faction labels use display offsets, not moved geography. Faction labels disappear in the 85 km closer view. Dense legacy research labels remain a known limitation there.

## Evidence and date limitations

[Polybius 2.14–17, 19–21](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Polybius/2%2A.html) describes the Po geography, tribal sequence and the earlier Roman wars. His writing and the 225 campaign narrative are later than 278: relative regional associations support this audit, but do not establish exact 278 borders. Boii fighting in Etruria is campaign activity, not proof of annexation there.

[Livy 5.35](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Liv.%205.35) associates Cenomani with Brixia and Verona; it is a later literary account of earlier migration. [Patavium, Oxford Classical Dictionary](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/549692743?login=false) identifies the Venetic city and its resistance to a Spartan attack in 301 BCE, an earlier chronological anchor. [Strabo 4.6](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/e/roman/texts/strabo/4f%2A.html) distinguishes Alpine peoples but reflects a later world and even contains problematic river/lake identifications. It is evidence for research distinctions, not a ready-made 278 political map. [283 chronology](https://www.attalus.org/bc3/year283.html) provides the source trail for Boii/Etruscan campaigning and Roman expansion before the scenario date.

## Actual rendering pipeline

1. `godot-prototype/tools/faction-catalog-build.mjs` authors tribal `rows` as longitude/latitude rings. Initial faction labels, territories and controllers are separate fields. Northern tribal entries cite game rosters/regional associations, not scholarly frontier reconstructions.
2. `data/factions/scenario-278.json` stores those rings plus the dated Cliopatria source reconstructions. There are no researched province boundaries in this faction path; these are territory identities, not settlement-derived provinces. Simulation province concepts do not generate this political raster.
3. `tools/faction-map-raster.mjs` scan-converts rings into a 4096 x 2354 equirectangular ID image: x=(lon+12)/87, y=(62-lat)/50. Priority 100 overwrites provisional 50. Equal-priority sourced overlap becomes disputed ID 1; equal-priority provisional overlap is last-writer-wins, a confirmed order-dependent generation weakness. Sampled northern misassignments already occur in the input polygons, so rasterization is not their cause.
4. The same builder separately snaps label placements to their own raster ID on visible land. Those points are display anchors, not capitals, settlement coordinates, territory seeds or statements of total extent. Current Boii snapped label is approximately (11.205,44.508).
5. `faction_cartography.gd` loads IDs and controller palette separately. `political_map.gdshaderinc` uses geographic coordinates, the same bounds and north/south UV inversion. `world.gd` projects geography with a spherical azimuthal equidistant projection, and uses its inverse for navigation. `faction_overlay.gd` projects each label at terrain elevation through the camera. No evidence of a northern Italy axis inversion or faction-specific projection offset was found; ten polygon/raster probes agree. A full numerical projection/raster registration certification remains outside these probes.
6. Perspective pitch and relief can change apparent screen positions and occlusion; they cannot explain Patavium and Brixia already being assigned to the wrong polygons. The existing 60–180 km political transition replaces terrain with full palette fields. Border rendering deliberately suppresses the confidence distinction, exaggerating certainty.

Reproduce the independent coordinate probes with `node docs/northern-italy-audit/probe.mjs`; see `probes.json`. These geographic probes do not modify or endorse settlement catalog records. Source rings remain unchanged pending review.
