# Roma prototype assets

Downloaded 27 October 2026. Only six 1K JPG maps, approximately 5 MB combined; no asset packs, proprietary game models, paid assets or runtime services.

* **Plastered Stone Wall**, Rob Tuytel, [exact asset](https://polyhaven.com/a/plastered_stone_wall): color, OpenGL normal and roughness. The asset page explicitly identifies CC0. Used as weathered wall-surface reference, not an authenticated ancient material sample.
* **Clay Roof Tiles**, Amal Kumar, [exact asset](https://polyhaven.com/a/clay_roof_tiles): color, OpenGL normal and roughness. Asset page explicitly identifies CC0. Contemporary photographic surface used interpretively on original early Italic architectural forms; not evidence for a particular tile workshop or roof in 278 BCE.

## Historical guardrails

* [Capitoline Museums: Temple of Capitoline Jupiter](https://museicapitolini.org/en/sede/campidoglio_antico/tempio_di_giove_capitolino): archaic temple inaugurated in 509 BCE; supports a Tuscan-style civic/religious landmark before 278 BCE. The model is a schematic early temple, not a faithful reconstruction of the actual Capitoline siting, ornament or building phases.
* [Roma tourism authority: temple](https://www.turismoroma.it/en/node/1106): roughly 53 × 63 m, high podium and frontal access; these support the model's broad proportions.
* [Roma archaeological authority: Servian walls](https://www.sovraintendenzaroma.it/node/109456): surviving large tuff-block defences mostly derive from fourth-century BCE rebuilding. A short plain tuff boundary fragment is interpretive; no Aurelian wall, medieval towers or claimed surveyed circuit.
* [Archaic domestic remains on the north Palatine slope, Journal of Roman Archaeology](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-roman-archaeology/article/monumental-archaic-complex-on-the-sacra-via-a-new-interpretation-of-the-domestic-remains-on-the-north-palatine-slope/D91F0DDBFA0F3B283264A8ACD2ED9953): courtyard-wing forms in central Italian archaic domestic architecture support the courtyard typology. They do not establish this illustrative street plan or the exact mix of surviving houses in 278 BCE.

Workshop, raised storehouse, shops, longhouse and peripheral round structures are illustrative vernacular categories, not individually attested Roma building identifications. No Imperial basilica, Colosseum, triumphal arch, monumental aqueduct or later marble forum was added. The compact displayed footprint is a campaign-map convention, not the full historical urban extent. No population is inferred.
